# Reference-informed design

Forma 1.4.0 researches public Dribbble work, studies actual reference images, proposes three original concepts and reviews the rendered result. The current brief and preservation constraints come first, required brand identity second, and compatible personal taste third.

The research pass searches sector, medium and visual approach rather than client names, exact copy or contact details. Search snippets alone do not count as opened references. Available public artwork is fetched directly on the user's computer. Each attached image has a required artistic study: a visible decision, a transferable principle and what must not be copied. Two credited visual studies catalogued on October 15, 2026 are available when live image discovery fails. They are references, not evidence of a current market trend.

The art director also sees the client's supplied photographs, including assets retained from an approved proposal. Direction, composition and critique share the same commission: request, dimensions, audience, style, brand, palette, campaign brief, personal preferences and explicit outcome/message/preserve/avoid fields. Direction specifies the first and supporting read, image relationship, type roles and a feasible visual idea. It is constrained to the actual renderer's available layers and effects. For a revision, its alternatives stay inside the approved concept and requested change.

The composition pass produces editable layers. It has no prescribed layer count or mandatory headline/photo/detail recipe. Density follows the job; supporting elements must help communication, grouping or legibility. Personal taste is translated into craft, while brand mission guides relevance and tone. Neither is permission to add slogans, arbitrary motifs or unrelated decoration. The designer can intentionally crop photos around a focal position while preserving the original source and aspect ratio. Logos, stickers and preserved reference artwork stay contained. Reference identities and artwork never become canvas assets.

A separate AI pass critiques actual previews made by the editor's Fabric renderer, with additional previews reduced to a maximum edge of 320 pixels. It checks the commission, reading order, image/type relationship, hierarchy, legibility, copy fit, contrast, crop, spacing, overlap, relevance and repeated layouts. Readable but arbitrary decoration can still require correction. One craft revision is allowed, followed by another rendered review. The repair receives the actual current-page images as well as specific feedback and existing editable layers; it is instructed to preserve successful relationships. Image data is attached as vision input and excluded from textual layout metrics.

Open **AI Designer → Results → Design direction & review** for the chosen concept, original twist, source credits and review status. Image studies and description studies are labelled separately. If research or rendering is unavailable, the result identifies the limitation and retains a validated editable design. If a revision cannot be verified, the original is preserved. Remaining review issues stay visible. Jobs remain cancellable and have no fixed generation deadline.

This is an AI craft review, not human approval, an objective quality score or a promise of marketing performance. Orchestration and designs stay on the user's device; AI requests use their own Codex account and provider usage. Public references need internet access. No Forma-operated design server is required.

These changes improve the existing workflow rather than introducing a replacement design system. They follow the [official OpenAI guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latest-model?model=gpt-6.1-sol) to use precise instructions and validate model behavior on representative application tasks. Automated contract and browser checks verify context retention, editing boundaries, thumbnail rendering and saved preferences; they cannot establish subjective professional quality. Real rendered examples must also be inspected.

The fallback studies are [Phren Craft](https://dribbble.com/shots/27416611-Phren-Craft-Celtic-Modular-Identity-System) by Ivana Mundja and [Cubee](https://dribbble.com/shots/26805880-Cubee-Visual-Identity-Toolkit-Brand-System) by Dtail Branding for Dtail Studio. Their material-related colors, typographic hierarchy and coherent systems are studied; their identities, symbols and layouts must not be reproduced.
